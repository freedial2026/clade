### Decisions and risks

- Confidence intervals use a normal approximation (sample stderr × a
  z-score) rather than bootstrapping or a t-distribution, to stay
  dependency-free (no scipy) and simple to audit; this under-covers true
  uncertainty for very small n — acceptable here since real subgroup
  sizes don't exist yet to judge against.
- Only four confidence levels are supported (80/90/95/99%) via a fixed
  z-score lookup table rather than an inverse-normal-CDF computation;
  documented as a deliberate simplicity/dependency tradeoff, easy to
  extend if another level is needed.
- This computes the report only — it does not itself gate anything
  (e.g. does not call into `quality.py`'s `require_allowed`); wiring a
  flagged concentration or wide CI into an actual decision is left for
  when this runs against real paper-simulation output.
